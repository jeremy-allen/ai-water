Method and boundaries

How the square areas were calculated

The 620-unit farm-irrigation square defines the reference area. Each other side equals 620 multiplied by the square root of that category’s annual volume divided by 99.9 trillion liters.

side = 620 × √(category volume ÷ 99.9 trillion L)

Farm and golf figures report water applied. The EPA residential figures report water use. The residential outdoor estimate covers outdoor water use mainly for landscape irrigation, so the estimate is broader than lawn irrigation alone. The data-center figure reports onsite water consumption, which Berkeley Lab defines as water removed from the immediate water cycle through evaporation or another irreversible process. These accounting boundaries are related but not identical.

Farm irrigation, residential outdoor water use, residential showers, golf courses, and data centers do not form a part-to-whole hierarchy. The AI estimate uses AI’s upper-bound 20% share of data-center electricity as a proxy for 20% of total data-center onsite water consumption. The separate layout prevents the comparison from implying a nested hierarchy.

Evidence

Sources and derivation notes

The listed values come from the source documents below. The notes show the conversions and the AI estimate so readers can reproduce each number.

  1. USDA National Agricultural Statistics Service, 2023 Irrigation and Water Management Survey. USDA Table 7 reports 81,005,833 acre-feet of water applied from all sources in 2023. Using 1 acre-foot = 1,233,481.84 liters gives 99.919 trillion liters, rounded here to 99.9 trillion. Read USDA Table 7.
  2. Golf Course Superintendents Association of America, Water Use and Conservation Practices. The 2025 report estimates that U.S. golf facilities applied 1,631,915 acre-feet in 2024. The converted volume is 2.013 trillion liters, rounded here to 2.01 trillion. Read the GCSAA report.
  3. U.S. Environmental Protection Agency WaterSense, Outdoor Water Use in the United States. EPA estimates that residential outdoor water use totals nearly 8 billion gallons each day nationwide, mainly for landscape irrigation. Multiplying 8 billion gallons by 365 days and 3.785411784 liters per gallon gives 11.053 trillion liters per year, rounded here to 11.1 trillion. The EPA category includes more than lawn irrigation alone. Read the EPA factsheet.
  4. U.S. Environmental Protection Agency WaterSense, Showerheads. EPA estimates that U.S. showers use nearly 1.2 trillion gallons per year. Multiplying that volume by 3.785411784 liters per gallon gives 4.542 trillion liters, rounded here to 4.54 trillion. The CBS News comparison reports 1.7 trillion gallons but does not identify a row-specific source, so this page uses the EPA estimate. Read the EPA shower estimate. See the CBS News comparison.
  5. Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, 2024 United States Data Center Energy Usage Report. The report estimates 66 billion liters of direct onsite water consumption and nearly 800 billion liters of indirect electricity-related water consumption in 2023. See the report’s water discussion on pages 55–58. Read the Berkeley Lab report page.
  6. Electric Power Research Institute, Powering Intelligence, May 2024. EPRI estimates that AI applications used 10–20% of data-center electricity at the time of publication. This page applies the 20% upper bound to Berkeley Lab’s onsite water total. The resulting 13.2 billion liters is rounded to 13 billion liters. This proportional assignment is an illustrative estimate, not a measured water result. Read the EPRI report.
  7. Berkeley Lab, The water use of data center workloads, June 2025. The peer-reviewed assessment finds more than 10,000-fold variation in workload-level water consumption across combinations of server efficiency, grid mix, utilization, cooling system, climate, and other determinants. The result limits what an electricity-share assumption can establish about AI water consumption. Read the Berkeley Lab publication page.
  8. U.S. Government Accountability Office, GAO-25-107172, April 2025. GAO reports that commercial developers have not released training-water totals and that company reports do not categorize data-center water consumption by computation type. AI-specific water consumption is therefore not directly measured. Read the GAO report.
